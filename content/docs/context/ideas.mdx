---
title: Idea Inbox
description: Where a thought goes when it is not ready to be used.
---

A notepad that lives with the repository. `IDEAS.md` sits at the root of the
repository and holds anything not yet worth deciding: half-formed thoughts,
questions with no owner, directions that might matter later.

<Callout title="Nothing reads this file">
  An agent will not see anything in `IDEAS.md` on its own, and nothing in the
  inbox has to be processed. An idea enters the work when you point at one.
</Callout>

## API

| Operation | Input | Output | Skill |
| --------- | ----- | ------ | ----- |
| **Capture** | a thought | an entry | `vatras:idea` |
| **Critique** | an entry | objections, hidden assumptions, alternatives | `vatras:critique` |
| **Investigate** | an entry + open questions | findings, constraints | `vatras:investigate` |
| **Iterate** | an entry | a sharper entry | `vatras:iterate` |
| **Duplicates** | the inbox | duplicate ideas | `vatras:duplicates` |
| **Develop** | an entry | critique + investigation + iteration | `vatras:develop` |

Each operation is independent. Use one operation directly or combine them with
`vatras:develop`.

## Explanation

### Capture

Capture turns the thought into an idea:

```text
vatras:idea "We could use the old mine to get behind the orcs."
```

The skill creates the entry and gives it an id and timestamp:

```text
## 0023 — 2026-08-13

We could use the old mine to get behind the orcs.
```

An entry is an id, a date, and whatever you had in your head at the
time. The id identifies the idea and never changes.

## Mental model

An idea is something you want to write down and come back to later.

You're working on something, a thought occurs to you, and you don't want
to stop and explore it. You just want to get it out of your head and
carry on.

An idea is not a task, a plan, or a decision. Writing something down does not
mean that it will be built.

### Where ideas live

Ideas live in `IDEAS.md` at the root of the repository, beside the code.

It is a personal notepad, not project context. Nothing reads it automatically
and nothing in it creates an obligation to act.

That is deliberate.

An inbox where every entry must be processed is a queue. A queue creates
obligation. The Idea Inbox does not.

### From thought to work

The overall model is:

```text
                         a thought
                             │
                          capture
                             │
                             ▼
                          ┌─────┐
                          │idea │
                          └──┬──┘
                             │
                ┌────────────┼────────────┐
                │            │            │
                ▼            ▼            ▼
             critique    investigate    iterate
                │            │            │
                └────────────┼────────────┘
                             │
                             └───────────↺
                             │
                             ▼
                           decide
                        ┌────┼────┐
                        │    │    │
                        ▼    ▼    ▼
                      plan merge drop
                        │
                        ▼
                       build
                        │
                        ▼
                      verify
```

This is a model of what can happen, not a workflow every idea must follow.

An idea might be captured and left alone. It might be investigated
immediately. It might be critiqued, split into two ideas, iterated several
times, or dropped as soon as it is understood.

The loop exists because understanding can change the idea.

### Working on an idea

When you want to think about an idea, point an operation at its id.

```text
0023
```

Operations write what they produce back into the entry. The idea therefore
becomes richer without becoming a task or leaving the inbox.

For example:

```text
## 0023 — 2026-08-13

We could use the old mine to get behind the orcs.
The mine has been sealed since the flood, and nobody has been to the far end.
```

Critique, investigation, and iteration can happen in any order. None is
mandatory.

Ideas can also produce ideas. If one idea turns out to contain two, it can be
split. If asking for alternatives produces several distinct possibilities,
those can become entries too.

`Duplicates` works differently: it considers the whole inbox. Ideas repeat
because nobody rereads them, and the same thought written twice at different
times can look like two ideas until they are compared.

### The decision boundary

An idea ends when you decide what to do with it.

The normal path into work is:

```text
idea → plan → build → verify
```

A plan is the boundary because an idea says **what you might want**, while a
plan decides **what you are going to pursue and how**.

The exit is not always a plan. An idea can also merge into another idea or be
dropped. The resolution is recorded so the id remains valid and nothing that
referred to it is left pointing at a gap.

The Idea Inbox owns the **idea**. Once it becomes a plan, it leaves the inbox
and enters [the methodology](/docs/methodology).
